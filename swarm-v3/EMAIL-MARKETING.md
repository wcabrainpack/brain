## 1. The Daily Email Doctrine

### Why You Must Email Every Single Day

Jeremiah is absolute about this: **email your list at least once a day, sometimes twice a day.** He has sent emails twice a day to his list and had people open every single one. The logic is simple — out of sight, out of mind. If you email once a week or once a month, you are invisible to your audience.

> "One of the biggest mistakes that people make with their email marketing is they email their list once a week, or they emailed their list once a month, because they don't want to send too many emails to people, and unfortunately, you are out of sight, out of mind to most people."

> "Sending less emails is not an answer to get them to not ignore or to listen to you more. Just doesn't work that way."

**The core philosophy:**
- Expect most people to ignore most of your emails. That's normal.
- Emailing less does NOT fix low engagement. It makes it worse.
- The people who DO open your emails — those are your people. They will become your clients.
- Consistency beats perfection. A mediocre email sent today outperforms a perfect email never sent.

### How to Never Run Out of Email Content

Jeremiah's content sources for daily emails:

1. **YouTube videos** — Send an email summarizing or teasing a recent video
2. **Podcast episodes** — Broadcast new episodes to your list
3. **Stories from your life** — Personal anecdotes connected to a lesson
4. **Client wins and case studies** — Social proof wrapped in narrative
5. **Industry observations** — Your take on what's happening in your market
6. **Repurposed social media content** — Turn a post into a longer email
7. **Lead magnets and free resources** — Broadcast free value
8. **Behind-the-scenes moments** — "I was with my kids and got 3 course sales"

> "I try to broadcast my list at least once a day, sometimes twice a day."

### The Mindset Shift

Stop overthinking. Stop trying to make every email perfect. Jeremiah admits he himself has struggled with overthinking emails:

> "I've over thought about this, and I put way too much pressure to make the emails perfect, and what I find is that that is just way more detrimental to our goals, to our businesses, to what we want to accomplish, than actually putting our best foot forward."

> "The worst thing you can do is not send emails out to people. The most detrimental thing that you can do is not email your email list."

**Rule:** A sent email always beats an unsent email. Hit send.

## 2. The Three Email Types

Jeremiah breaks all email marketing into three categories. Everything you send falls into one of these:

### Type 1: Broadcast Emails

**What they are:** One-time emails sent to your entire list (or a segment). These are not automated — you write and send them in real time.

**When to send:** At least once per day, sometimes twice per day.

**What to include in broadcasts:**
- YouTube video promotions
- Podcast episode announcements
- Lead magnet offers
- Event invitations
- Stories with a lesson and a CTA
- Direct sales pitches for your program
- Behind-the-scenes content
- Industry commentary

**How Jeremiah structures his broadcast list:**
> "I'll have my entire list in one bucket where I can send emails to everybody all at once when they come in."

### Type 2: Autoresponder Series

**What they are:** Pre-written sequences of automated emails triggered when someone takes a specific action (usually opting in for a lead magnet). These are evergreen — it doesn't matter when someone enters the sequence, they get the same emails.

**Standard Jeremiah autoresponder structure (4-day sequence):**

| Timing | Email Count | Purpose |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Day 1 (opt-in)** | 1 email | Thank them, deliver the lead magnet |
| **Day 2** | 2 emails | Remind them what they signed up for + sales pitch |
| **Day 3** | 2 emails | Another reminder + sales pitch |
| **Day 4 (deadline)** | 3 emails | Final urgency — 3 emails leading up to deadline |

> "On each of these days, I will send out something that reminds them of what they signed up for, and then I might also send them an offer that's a sales pitch to my program."

**What goes inside autoresponder emails:**
- Reminders of what they signed up for
- Stories and personal anecdotes
- Testimonials and case studies
- Reaffirming why they made the right choice opting in
- Direct sales pitches for your paid program
- Deadline/urgency elements (countdown timers, expiring bonuses)

**Deadline Funnel tip:** Even if your offer never expires, create artificial urgency. Use Deadline Funnel or a countdown timer to make people feel a time constraint. This dramatically increases conversions.

> "Even if you don't have an offer that's expiring, even if it doesn't expire forever, you could say that there's a limited time for you to jump in and just have a countdown timer of some kind."

### Type 3: Event Series Emails

**What they are:** Emails sent before and after a live event (webinar, challenge, masterclass). These are date-specific — the event is on a fixed day for everyone. It's a hybrid between broadcast and autoresponder.

**The event email sequence has two phases:**

#### Phase A: Pre-Event Sequence

**After registration (4 reminder emails):**
1. **Immediately after sign-up** — Confirmation: thank them, give them the details (time, Zoom link, bonus info)
2. **Day 1 post-registration** — "Hey, excited to have you at the event"
3. **Day 2 post-registration** — Another reminder with a different angle
4. **Day 3 post-registration** — Continue building anticipation

**Leading up to event day (5 emails in 24 hours):**

| Timing | Subject Line | Purpose |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Day before** | "Training starts tomorrow at 2pm" | Final reminder with details |
| **Morning of** | Day-of reminder | Block out time, get ready |
| **1 hour before** | "Going live in one hour" | Zoom link + 60-second quick prep |
| **15 minutes before** | "We're starting in 15 minutes" | Final prep, join 5 minutes early |
| **At event start** | "We are live now" | Direct link to join the live room |

> "They get five emails over the course of 24 hours from me right there, and I'll tell you this, after pushing thousands of people through offers like live events like this, not one person... maybe one person... out of thousands unsubscribed, and zero complaints."

**The critical insight most people miss:**
> "I've met a lot of people that don't send enough emails that lead up to their event, and then they're like, nobody showed up. Well, you had an option. You had a choice that you made. Did not send enough emails."

#### Phase B: Post-Event Sequence

Immediately after the event ends, launch a follow-up sales sequence:

| Timing | Email Count | Purpose |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Immediately after event** | 1 | Recap with bullet points + replay link |
| **Day after event** | 2 | Replay reminder + offer pitch |
| **Day 2 after event** | 2 | More about the offer that was sold |
| **Day 3 after event** | 2 | Offer + replay, "final day to get the recording" |
| **Final day (deadline)** | 3 | Last chance emails, including last 3 hours |

> "On the final day, three emails about the offer, with on the last three hours leading up to when the doors close."

## 3. Subject Line Formulas That Work

Jeremiah doesn't teach abstract copywriting theory for subject lines — he teaches a practical method: **scroll through your swipe file and notice which subject lines make YOU stop.**

Here are subject line patterns he references from real marketers:

### Curiosity Gap
- "No list? No problem."
- "How does $60,000 in four hours sound?"
- "You might be wondering this..."

### Direct Benefit
- "Training starts tomorrow at 2pm"
- "Going live in one hour"
- "We are live now"
- "Replay ready"

### Story Tease
- "I spent the summer hooked on Olympic coverage"
- "An Olympic-size story for you"
- "The length of the master class depends on a number of factors"

### Pain Point Hook
- "Is it on a big list? Who's going to buy my offer?"
- "Tired of feeling tired?"

### Personal / Casual
- "I want to honor your Monday inbox — I'll keep this short"
- Quick, casual, feels like a friend writing

**Jeremiah's method for finding subject lines:**
> "What I'm looking for when I want to write an email and I want to come up with a good subject line, I'll scroll through and I'll see which ones stop me, which ones give me a pause."

**Key principle:** Subject lines that feel personal and curiosity-driven outperform ones that feel like marketing. Write subject lines the way you'd text a friend.

## 4. The Story → Bridge → CTA Structure

This is the fundamental email writing structure Jeremiah uses, whether he articulates it by name or not. Every effective email follows this pattern:

### Step 1: Story
Open with a personal story, an observation, or a real-life moment. This is what hooks people.

**Example from Jeremiah's teaching:**
He describes writing an email about "Sarah, mom of three, who used to hit the afternoon wall so hard she'd hide in her car during her lunch breaks." That's the story — specific, visual, relatable.

Another example: "I was spending time with my kids and my business was making sales automatically." That's a story that teaches time freedom.

### Step 2: Bridge
Connect the story to the reader's situation. Show them this isn't just YOUR story — it's THEIR problem too.

**Example:**
> "In fact, it struck me that many of us have the same exhausting story. There's the third cup of coffee by noon, the brain fog during important meetings, the guilty feeling that we're too tired to play with our kids after work."

The bridge says: "This isn't just about Sarah. This is about YOU."

### Step 3: Call to Action (CTA)
Every email has ONE clear action. Not two. Not three. One.

> "There's a small call to action at the bottom of it, for a lead magnet, for a challenge, for an event, for an offer, for a course or a coaching program."

**CTA examples:**
- "Join us for this event — choose the session that works best for you"
- "Click here to watch the replay"
- "Sign up before midnight — first 20 participants get a complimentary consultation valued at $250"
- "The doors close at midnight — click here to join now"

**Critical rule:** Each email must have ONE clear purpose and one CTA. Never mix multiple CTAs.

### Full Story → Bridge → CTA Example (Rewritten from Jeremiah's AI session):

> **Subject:** Remember when you could make it through the day without crashing?
>
> **Story:** Last week I was talking about Sarah, mom of three, who used to hit the afternoon wall so hard she'd hide in her car during her lunch breaks.
>
> **Bridge:** You see, the desire to feel alive again must be stronger than accepting this as your new normal. If you're like most women I've talked to, you've probably tried every energy boosting supplement on the market, going to bed earlier, cutting sugar, even that expensive miracle vitamin that did nothing. You're still dragging yourself through each day wondering why nothing works.
>
> **CTA:** Here's what nobody tells you — it's not your fault, and there IS a scientific reason nothing has worked. Join us for our hormone health master class this Thursday and discover the real solution. [Link]

## 5. The AI Swipe File Method

This is Jeremiah's signature method for writing emails faster using AI (specifically Claude). It's a three-step process:

### Step 1: Find a Killer Email in Your Swipe File
Go to your dedicated swipe file email account. Search for a specific marketer or scroll through recent emails. Find one that catches your attention — a great subject line, a compelling story, a strong CTA.

### Step 2: Paste It Into Claude With a Rewrite Prompt
Copy the entire email and give Claude a specific prompt:

**Basic rewrite prompt:**
> "Rewrite the following email to promote [your specific offer/product/service]: [paste email]"

**Advanced rewrite prompt:**
> "Rewrite the email above as a direct-to-consumer email, same tone and cadence, to promote an event and offer to [become a patient / join the program / sign up for coaching]"

**Pain-point focused prompt:**
> "Make this more persuasive and focused on a top pain point [your audience] deals with, to promise one big outcome"

**Style-matching prompt:**
> "Write an email based on the above topic, but use the following as a framework for the tone, style and cadence: [paste a different email you admire]"

### Step 3: Edit and Personalize
The AI gives you a strong first draft. Now you go in and:
- Adjust the tone to match YOUR voice
- Add personal details and real stories
- Swap generic examples for specific ones from your business
- Tighten the language

> "What I would do is I would go in and I would start to rewrite whatever the AI is writing. And then what this does is it allows you to develop your own tone, your own style, your own cadence."

### Using AI to Brainstorm Email Angles

Not sure what to write about? Ask Claude:
> "Brainstorm 20 different email angles for promoting [your event/offer]"

Then pick the best angle and ask Claude to generate the email. Angles might include:
- **Time angle** — "How much time are you wasting doing X the wrong way?"
- **Social proof angle** — "Sarah did X and got Y result in Z days"
- **Market demand angle** — "The industry is shifting — here's what's working now"
- **Pain point angle** — "Tired of [specific frustration]? Here's what nobody tells you"
- **Behind-the-scenes angle** — "Here's what my business looks like on a Tuesday"

### Using AI to Review and Improve Your Emails

After writing (or AI-generating) an email, ask Claude:
> "Review this email and provide improvement recommendations"

Then apply the feedback. Common AI recommendations Jeremiah applies:
- Reduce statistics if they feel overwhelming
- Simplify language
- Add more FOMO (fear of missing out)
- Make the CTA more direct
- Cut unnecessary testimony
- Strengthen the opening hook

### Why Claude (Not GPT) for Emails

> "GPT-4 is great, but it's not the best at writing emails. Claude Sonnet 3.5... Claude is the one that you want to use. In our testing, we've noticed a lot of the AIs aren't that great at writing emails the way they need to be written. But except for Claude, which is, like, out of the box, incredible when it comes to writing email marketing."

Jeremiah uses GPT for sales copy and Facebook ads, Meta's Llama for Facebook ads specifically, but Claude for all email marketing.

## 6. The 5-7 Day Welcome Sequence

This is Jeremiah's autoresponder sequence that fires after someone opts in for a lead magnet (free ebook, webinar, challenge, etc.). Every person who enters your list goes through this.

### Day 1 — Opt-In Day (1 Email)

**Purpose:** Thank them and deliver the goods.

**Structure:**
- Congratulate them on signing up
- Deliver the lead magnet (link, PDF, video access)
- Set expectations: "You'll be hearing from me regularly"
- Soft introduction to who you are

**Subject line idea:** "Here's your [lead magnet name] — plus a welcome gift"

### Day 2 — Reminder + First Pitch (2 Emails)

**Email 1 — Reminder:**
- Remind them what they signed up for (most ignored Day 1)
- Share a quick insight or tip from the lead magnet
- Encourage them to actually consume it

**Email 2 — First Sales Pitch:**
- Introduce your paid program/offer
- Connect it to the problem they're trying to solve
- Clear CTA: "Click here to learn more about [program name]"

### Day 3 — Story + Sales (2 Emails)

**Email 1 — Client Story:**
- Share a case study or testimonial
- "Meet [client name] — they had [problem] and after [joining our program] they got [result]"
- Tie it back to the reader's situation

**Email 2 — Direct Sales Pitch:**
- Different angle than yesterday's pitch
- Focus on a specific benefit or outcome
- Include a deadline or urgency element

### Day 4 — Reinforcement + Sales (2 Emails)

**Email 1 — Reinforcement:**
- Remind them of the lead magnet value
- Share another insight or "aha" moment
- "Here's something most people miss from the [lead magnet]..."

**Email 2 — Sales:**
- Address common objections
- "You might be thinking [objection] — here's why that's not true"
- Reiterate the transformation

### Day 5-7 — Deadline Sequence (3 Emails on Final Day)

**Email 1 (Morning):** "Last chance — [offer] closes tonight at midnight"
**Email 2 (Afternoon):** "Just a few hours left — here's what happens if you don't join"
**Email 3 (Final 3 hours):** "Doors closing — this is your last email about [offer]"

> "Whatever I'm selling, whatever that sales offer is, I'm going to talk about it three times, and I'm going to send an email out at the very end leading up to midnight, when the doors close."

**Note on longer sequences:** Some people run 21-day autoresponder sequences. The same principles apply — stories, testimonials, case studies, reaffirming why they signed up, reminding them of what they signed up for, and making offers.

Jeremiah has refined this through thousands of live event registrations. Here is the complete sequence, every email in order:

## 8. MailerLite Setup and Segmentation Strategy

Jeremiah uses a three-bucket segmentation system. Regardless of the email platform (he references MailerLite and MailChimp), the structure is the same:

### Bucket 1: Your Entire List
- Everyone who has ever opted in
- Receives daily broadcast emails
- Content: YouTube videos, podcasts, lead magnets, general offers, stories
- This is your main audience

### Bucket 2: Lead Magnet / Autoresponder Segments
- People who signed up for a specific lead magnet
- Receive the automated 4-7 day autoresponder sequence
- Once the sequence ends, they merge into Bucket 1
- You can have multiple lead magnet segments running simultaneously

### Bucket 3: Course / Coaching Program Members
- People who have purchased from you
- Receive course-specific emails: call reminders, new content alerts, program updates
- Sent once or twice per week (less frequent than broadcasts)
- Do NOT send general sales pitches to people who already bought

### Smart Segmentation Rules:

1. **Don't send event invites to people already registered** — Segment them out. If someone signed up for your webinar, remove them from the broadcast list for that specific event promotion.

2. **Tag people based on actions** — Who opens your emails? Who clicks? Those tags let you send targeted offers to your most engaged subscribers.

3. **Segment by interest** — If someone signed up for a lead magnet about "getting more clients," don't send them emails about "productivity hacks." Stay relevant.

> "If I'm broadcasting people about the event, I don't necessarily want to send them another email about that event that I'm broadcasting, if they've already signed up for the event. So we can segment people out that way."

## 9. Common Email Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Not Sending Enough Emails
> "The worst thing you can do is not send emails out to people."

Emailing once a week or once a month because you're afraid of bothering people is the #1 mistake. You are doing your audience a disservice by not showing up.

### Mistake 2: Overthinking and Perfectionism
> "I put way too much pressure to make the emails perfect, and what I find is that that is just way more detrimental."

Perfect is the enemy of sent. Write it, review it once, send it.

### Mistake 3: Multiple Calls to Action in One Email
Every email must have ONE clear purpose. Don't pitch your course, link to your YouTube video, and promote your webinar all in the same email. Pick one.

### Mistake 4: Not Using Deadlines and Urgency
Even if your offer is evergreen and never expires, create urgency. Use Deadline Funnel, countdown timers, or expiring bonuses. People need a reason to act NOW.

### Mistake 5: Not Reminding People Enough Before Events
> "I've met a lot of people that don't send enough emails that lead up to their event, and then they're like, nobody showed up."

Five emails in the 24 hours before a live event is not excessive — it's the minimum for strong attendance.

### Mistake 6: Fear of Unsubscribes
> "We'll get some unsubscribes. There'll be people that don't want to receive the emails, but that's okay. We let them go."

Unsubscribes are part of email marketing. They are NOT a sign you're doing something wrong. The people who stay are your real audience.

### Mistake 7: Not Building a Swipe File
Without a swipe file, you're writing every email from scratch. With one, you have an endless source of inspiration and proven structures to learn from.

### Mistake 8: Using the Wrong AI for Emails
> "A lot of the AIs aren't that great at writing emails the way they need to be written. But except for Claude."

Use Claude (Anthropic) for email writing. Use GPT for sales copy and Facebook ads. Use the right tool for the right job.

## 10. How to Build a Swipe File Email Account

This is one of Jeremiah's most valuable teachings. Here's the complete system:

### Step 1: Create a Dedicated Gmail Account
Create a Gmail account specifically for receiving marketing emails. This account has ONE purpose: collecting emails from other marketers and companies.

> "Have an email account that is dedicated towards spam — dedicated towards marketing emails from marketers, from people who are marketing to you."

Jeremiah's own swipe file account is 15+ years old and contains emails from thousands of marketers.

### Step 2: Get on Other People's Email Lists

**Method A — Click Facebook Ads:**
1. Browse Facebook/Instagram and look for ads in your niche or related niches
2. Click on the ads
3. Opt into whatever they're offering (lead magnet, webinar, free trial)
4. Use your swipe file email address when signing up

**Method B — Engage with Content to Trigger Ads:**
1. Visit 10+ websites related to your target market
2. Follow their social media pages
3. Like and engage with their content
4. This triggers the Facebook/Instagram pixel to start showing you their ads
5. Then click those ads and opt in

> "Start going around the internet and looking at content on as many websites as possible around the topic that you want to learn from."

**Method C — Direct Opt-In:**
1. Go to competitor websites
2. Sign up for their newsletters, lead magnets, and free offers
3. Join their Facebook groups
4. Follow their funnels from start to finish

**Method D — Look at Pages Running Ads:**
1. Visit a competitor's Facebook page
2. Click "Page Transparency" → "See All" → "Go to Ad Library"
3. See all ads they're currently running
4. Click through and opt into their funnels

### Step 3: Organize Your Swipe File

- **By sender:** Search by email address to see everything one marketer sends
  - Example: Search "Eileen Wilder" to see her complete email cadence over months
- **By date:** Scroll through daily to see what's landing in your inbox
- **By subject line:** Notice which subject lines make you stop scrolling — those are the winners

### Step 4: Use Your Swipe File Daily

When you need to write an email:
1. Open your swipe file account
2. Search for a marketer you admire or scroll recent emails
3. Find an email that catches your attention
4. Copy it into Claude
5. Use the AI Swipe File Method (Section 5) to rewrite it for your audience
6. Edit to match your voice
7. Send

> "I can click on any number of these emails at any time... I can just go back day after day and find emails that are written by marketers that are sending just incredible content that I can dive into and look at and pull ideas from anytime I want."
