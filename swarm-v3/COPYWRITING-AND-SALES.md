## The Core Copywriting Standard

Jeremiah’s copywriting standard is simple: copy is not content. It is not there to impress, explain, or sound intellectual. It exists to influence action, create emotional certainty, and move the right person into the next step. The goal is not to perfectly describe a program. The goal is to make the right prospect feel seen, excited, and convinced that this offer is the bridge between where they are and what they want.

He repeatedly teaches that bad copy is usually too logical, too stiff, too careful, too explanatory, or too obsessed with sounding polished. Good copy feels alive. It sounds like movement. It creates desire. It paints a result. It mirrors pain. It pulls the reader emotionally toward a decision.

He also treats copy as a filtering system. Great copy attracts the right clients and repels the wrong ones. That means clarity matters more than pleasing everyone. The copy must qualify, not just persuade.

### Name
The Emotional Outcome Standard

### Step-by-step process
1. Start with the result the prospect wants, not the method you use.
2. Identify the pain, frustration, confusion, or cost of staying where they are.
3. Put language to that pain in the prospect’s own words.
4. Paint the emotional outcome of crossing over into the result.
5. Position your offer as the next step toward that result.
6. Remove anything that sounds overly formal, informational, or “content-like.”
7. Judge the copy by how it feels, not just how correct it is.

### Example
Instead of saying, “This 8-week mentorship includes weekly coaching, templates, implementation support, and curriculum modules,” Jeremiah would lead with something closer to: “Start getting consistent clients without second-guessing your message, overcomplicating your funnel, or wasting money on content that never converts.”

The first version describes. The second version sells.

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“The worst thing that your sales copy can be is to inform people about what it is they’re signing up for. You want it to trigger an emotional response and speak to what people actually want.”

## The Rewrite Method

Jeremiah’s rewrite method is a layered refinement system. He does not expect the first draft from AI to be the final draft. He uses AI to generate raw material, then curates, sharpens, and reruns the best lines until the copy gets punchier, clearer, and more aligned with the prospect’s desire.

This method combines Dan Kennedy’s direct response foundations, Gary Halbert’s punch and headline instinct, and Sandy Krakowski’s market-tested emotional cadence. Jeremiah uses names the models recognize so the AI writes in a proven persuasive pattern instead of generic internet language.

He often uses three style anchors together: Dan Kennedy for direct-response structure, Gary Halbert for punch and hooks, and Sandy Krakowski for the tonal flavor that most closely resembles how he naturally writes sales copy.

### Name
The Dan Kennedy + Gary Halbert + Sandy Krakowski Rewrite Method

### Step-by-step process
1. Start with a rough sales letter, webinar page, email, or offer description.
2. Feed the AI market language first: pain points, objections, desires, survey responses, transcript snippets, customer wording, and smart market diagnosis details.
3. Ask the AI to rewrite the copy based on that customer language.
4. Do not rewrite the entire sales letter in one pass if quality drops. Rewrite one section at a time: headline, story section, CTA, bullets, email, or ad.
5. Prompt the AI to rewrite in the style or tone of Dan Kennedy, Gary Halbert, and Sandy Krakowski.
6. Generate multiple outputs, then copy all of them into a document.
7. Highlight only the top 10–20% of lines that actually hit.
8. Delete everything weak, repetitive, bland, or overly formal.
9. Feed the strongest lines back into the AI and have it rewrite again from the best parts only.
10. Repeat until the copy feels emotionally sharp, simple, and specific.

### Example
A rough webinar headline might begin as:

“Learn how to start your group mentorship business using AI.”

After survey-driven rewriting, it becomes:

“Start your group mentorship from scratch without feeling overwhelmed, confused by AI, or stuck picking the wrong niche.”

After another pass through the Kennedy/Halbert/Sandy filter, it becomes more emotionally alive:

“Stop sitting on the mentorship idea you know could change your business—get clear, get moving, and launch without drowning in tech confusion.”

The point is not one perfect prompt. The point is iterative pressure.

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“Go through the prompting, copy down the top 10 to 20% of the output it generated that you like, and then have it run the prompts again on that text, have it generate sales letters off of that text, and you’re going to notice your copy is going to get a lot stronger that way.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“I always like, filter all my sales copy to write it like, as Dan Kennedy, Gary Halbert, Sandy Krakowski.”

## Landing Page Anatomy

Jeremiah teaches that the landing page is the composition root of the whole marketing machine. The page determines the ad language, email language, social media messaging, and who the algorithms learn to send to the offer. The page must be launched quickly, then improved through iteration.

He favors simple pages: white background, black text, minimal design noise, strong headlines, clear story, a repeated call to action, and only enough visual support to guide attention. Fancy design is not the point. Communication is the point.

### Name
The Landing Page Blueprint

### Step-by-step process
1. Start in a Google Doc before touching the page builder.
2. Lead with a large headline at the top that clearly states the result.
3. Add a subtitle or context line that makes the promise clearer.
4. Include a graphic, simple image, or short video if it supports attention.
5. Move immediately into the story section—the emotional narrative about the person’s current struggle.
6. Validate that you understand the problem and can solve it.
7. Introduce your invitation: webinar, challenge, consultation, training, community, or paid next step.
8. Use bullets to clarify what they will get, learn, or experience.
9. Add proof elements: your track record, a case study, a testimony, or outcome evidence.
10. Place a clear call to action early and repeat it throughout the page.
11. Keep paragraphs short and skimmable.
12. Launch fast, then iterate from response data.

### Example
A Jeremiah-style webinar page often follows this flow:
- Headline: the promised result
- Subheadline: for who + by when or in what format
- Story section: “You know you’re called to this, but you’re stuck, overwhelmed, or using the wrong strategy”
- Track record and authority
- What they’ll learn
- Call to action button
- Optional repeated CTA lower on the page

For a coach, that means the page should not begin with curriculum or credentials. It should begin with the reader’s situation and desired result.

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“Every landing page we launch has a headline at the top.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“This is what I call the story section of the landing page, the story and so every landing page that we create has this structure right here in your copy.”

## Headline Formulas That Convert

Jeremiah treats the headline as the most important line on the page. If the headline misses, the rest of the page rarely gets read. He wants headlines to do three things quickly: stop attention, clarify the result, and trigger emotional relevance.

His best headline patterns are not clever for cleverness’ sake. They are direct, desire-led, and loaded with market language. The more specific the pain and the clearer the result, the stronger the headline.

### Name
The Headline and Hook Formula

### Step-by-step process
1. Lead with the end result people already want.
2. Add the biggest barrier, confusion, or frustration they are facing.
3. Use language they would actually say, not your internal terminology.
4. Make the promise bold, but understandable.
5. If possible, imply speed, simplicity, clarity, confidence, or relief.
6. Avoid vague spiritualized or brand-insider phrasing.
7. Test multiple versions and let engagement decide.

### Example
Jeremiah-style headline formulas:
- Start [desired result] without [biggest fear or obstacle]
- Get [result] even if [common objection]
- Stop [painful cycle] and finally [desired outcome]
- Learn how to [result] in [clear time frame or format]
- The simple path to [result] for [specific person]

Examples aligned with his transcripts:
- Start your six-figure group coaching business from scratch with AI
- Unlock the power of group mentorship with AI
- Start getting clients with simple, low-cost Instagram ads
- Feel confident leading worship instead of hiding in the shadows

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“The headline and hook is the most important part of your sales copy on any sales letter on any offer. It’s the single most important aspect.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“You want to be specific about the right things that your target client actually wants.”

## The Story Section: The Most Missed Element

Jeremiah repeatedly says most coaches miss the story section. They jump too quickly into features, modules, mechanics, or teaching points. But the story section is where the reader feels understood. It should tell the prospect’s story back to them in a way that creates emotional identification.

This section is not your life story unless your life story mirrors the prospect’s struggle in a useful way. The purpose is to narrate the reader’s pain, confusion, failed attempts, and desire for change so clearly that they feel: this person gets me.

### Name
The Prospect Storyback Section

### Step-by-step process
1. Identify the exact person the offer is for.
2. Describe what they are dealing with right now in emotionally recognizable language.
3. Name the confusion, overwhelm, false starts, or disappointment they are experiencing.
4. Contrast that with the result they secretly want.
5. Make the story short—usually two to three paragraphs is enough.
6. Transition from pain into possibility.
7. Move naturally into the invitation or proof.

### Example
For a coach helping overwhelmed mentors launch a group offer, the story section could sound like this:

You know you have something real to help people with, but every time you try to turn it into an offer, you get stuck. The tech feels confusing. The messaging feels too broad. You second-guess your niche, overthink your landing page, and wonder if anyone would actually pay for what you know how to do. You’ve probably bought courses, watched trainings, and tried piecing things together, but you still haven’t built the momentum you know you’re capable of.

That is classic Jeremiah copy logic: tell their story, name the emotional weight, then open the door.

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“What you want to do is you want to tell the story of your prospect back to them.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“The person who you’re looking to reach with your sales letter, wants to read their story told back to them in a way that shows that you understand what they’re going through.”

## Facebook and Instagram Ad Copy Patterns

Jeremiah does not separate ad copy from offer copy. He builds the landing page first, then converts the offer language into ads. He also prefers simple direct-response structures like problem-agitate-solution, and he often lets AI generate multiple ad angles from a stronger core sales letter.

His ads do not try to teach everything. They aim to stop the scroll, mirror pain, promise a result, and create curiosity or direct desire around the next step.

### Name
Offer-to-Ad Conversion Method

### Step-by-step process
1. Write the offer page or sales copy first.
2. Pull the strongest pain point, promise, or frustration into short-form ad language.
3. Convert the copy into PAS: problem, agitate, solution.
4. Create multiple ad angles from the same offer:
   - pain angle
   - desire angle
   - market opportunity angle
   - common mistake angle
   - confidence angle
5. Keep the ad focused on one emotional entry point.
6. Use a clear CTA: register, watch, comment, message, join, download.
7. Test several versions instead of trying to guess the winner.
8. Use organic-style language and simple visuals when possible.

### Example
A Jeremiah-style ad for a coach might sound like this:

Tired of overthinking your niche, your content, and your landing page—and still not getting clients?

You don’t need another year of “figuring it out.”

Join this free class and learn how to build a clear offer, write the copy, and launch it without drowning in tech confusion.

That ad is short, emotionally targeted, and action-driven.

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“Your Facebook ad is going to be based off of your offer copy, your social media content is going to be based off of your offer copy. Your emails are going to be based off of your offer copy.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“Chat GBT is really good at writing Facebook ads. We use it to write all of our Facebook ads.”

## Offer Positioning Language

Jeremiah teaches that the market does not buy your inside view of what you do. It buys a clear promise of change. This is why he constantly pushes coaches to stop describing their work as “a little group,” “Bible study,” “support,” “sessions,” or “what we cover.” That language minimizes perceived value.

The offer must be packaged around transformation, confidence, relief, progress, speed, or identity shift. He wants the positioning language to expand the emotional value of what is already happening inside the offer.

### Name
Transformation Packaging

### Step-by-step process
1. Identify the real result your work produces.
2. Translate that result into language the market already wants.
3. Remove minimizing language like “just,” “little,” or “kind of.”
4. Describe the identity shift or confidence shift created by your process.
5. Anchor the offer around the desire, not the mechanics.
6. Make the next step sound meaningful, not casual.

### Example
Weak positioning:
“I do a little Bible study group.”

Stronger positioning:
“I help worship leaders grow in confidence, leadership, and spiritual impact so they stop hiding and start leading with authority.”

Weak positioning:
“I teach ChatGPT and Facebook ads.”

Stronger positioning:
“I help coaches scale to seven figures by creating profitable offers, persuasive copy, and client-generating marketing systems.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“Nobody would buy it from me if I called it that.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“It’s how we package what we’re doing, amplifies the value.”

## Before and After Rewrite Patterns

Jeremiah’s live rewrites reveal a consistent pattern: the “before” version is usually vague, soft, overly broad, or concept-heavy. The “after” version becomes specific, emotional, easier to visualize, and more connected to the prospect’s lived reality.

He frequently adds texture, friction, and consequence. He also sharpens emotional contrast.

### Name
The Vague-to-Visceral Rewrite Pattern

### Step-by-step process
1. Find the sentence that feels flat or generic.
2. Replace broad concepts with real situations or feelings.
3. Add the actual friction the person experiences.
4. Shift from your summary to their lived experience.
5. Increase contrast between current pain and future result.
6. Remove filler words and weak abstractions.

### Example
Before:
“She lost herself in a toxic relationship and forgot who she was.”

After:
“She got into a toxic relationship and feels like the light she once carried got dimmed. Now she has to flick her flame back on.”

Before:
“Get clarity in your niche and learn how to use AI.”

After:
“Starting from scratch? Overwhelmed with AI? Learn how to gain momentum, choose the right niche, and stop getting stuck in confusion.”

Before:
“Do you want to feel better?”

After:
“Tired of feeling tired? Get your energy back without another cup of coffee.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“It needs a little bit more emotional language to it.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“We need to, like, identify the tension point, really, and the person’s actual experience that they’re having.”

## ChatGPT and Claude Prompts for Copy

Jeremiah uses AI as a collaborative copywriter, not a magic button. He feeds it structure, role, context, swipe examples, surveys, transcript language, and previous drafts. He also uses different models for different jobs: ChatGPT for sales copy and ideation, Claude for email writing and often subject lines.

### Name
The AI Prompt Stack for Copywriting

### Step-by-step process
1. Tell the AI what role to take.
   - “You are an email marketing expert.”
   - “Write a sales letter…”
2. Feed it offer context, target audience, and desired outcome.
3. Give it customer language from surveys, comments, transcripts, or intake forms.
4. Ask it to generate, rewrite, or improve one section at a time.
5. Use a style anchor when needed: Dan Kennedy, Gary Halbert, Sandy Krakowski.
6. Paste in a successful swipe file email or competitor email and ask it to rewrite your content in that tone, cadence, and structure.
7. Ask for multiple angles instead of one answer.
8. Simplify to a lower reading level if the output feels too polished or robotic.
9. Curate the strongest lines and rerun only the best pieces.

### Example
Useful Jeremiah-style prompt patterns:
- Rewrite the above sales letter based on the following survey answers and speak to their desires.
- Rewrite this section in the tone of Sandy Krakowski.
- Write me four versions of PAS ad copy from the sales letter above.
- Take the following email context above but rewrite it in the style, tone, and structure of the following template.
- Act as an expert in email marketing and direct response copywriting. Assess the email above, give improvement recommendations, and rewrite it.
- Simplify this to a fourth-grade reading level with words normal people understand.

Claude-specific use:
For email campaigns and subject lines, Jeremiah often prefers Claude because it writes with better rhythm and inbox tone.

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“You are an email marketing expert.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“Take the email context above, but rewrite it in the style, tone and structure of the following template.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“Chat GBT is not the best for subject lines… Claude is a little bit better for subject lines.”

## Common Copy Mistakes Jeremiah Critiques

Jeremiah’s critiques are remarkably consistent. Most struggling copy falls into a few repeated failure patterns: it is too generic, too broad, too methodical, too long without movement, too focused on process, too afraid to promise, or too packed with free value before the sale.

### Name
The Copy Mistake Checklist

### Step-by-step process
1. Remove generic language that could apply to anyone.
2. Stop trying to sound perfect or polished.
3. Cut repetition and over-explaining.
4. Stop leading with the method instead of the outcome.
5. Shorten anything that feels like “information” instead of persuasion.
6. Make sure the copy is specific about the right desire.
7. Do not overteach in free content or on the landing page.
8. Eliminate weak, minimizing, or hedged phrasing.
9. Keep the page simple—do not hide weak copy behind design.
10. Launch before perfecting.

### Example
Mistake: leading with curriculum
“Inside this training I’ll show you modules, templates, systems, automations, onboarding, and content planning.”

Better:
“Stop guessing what to sell and how to say it. Build a clear offer and start attracting clients who actually want it.”

Mistake: too broad
“I help women live their best life.”

Better:
“I help women recover confidence after toxic relationships so they stop living shut down and start feeling like themselves again.”

Mistake: overteaching free content
A 45-minute opt-in training that solves everything leaves no tension for the paid step.

Better: give clarity, momentum, and desire—then invite them deeper.

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“One issue that I see a lot of people make with their sales copy, they’re way too methodical with it. They’re way too rigid.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“This is an another huge mistake that I see all the time… all they’re leading with is the how to.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“You’re not specific enough, it’s too generic. It’s too general, it’s trying to reach way too many people all at once.”

## The “Who First” Principle Applied to Copy

Jeremiah’s copy framework is built on a who-first philosophy. Before clever writing, the coach must know who the copy is for. Not in vague demographic terms alone, but in terms of desire, buying behavior, pain language, and emotional context.

The wrong who creates weak copy, weak ads, weak offers, and weak conversions. The right who makes good copy easier, because the language becomes obvious.

### Name
Who First, Copy Second

### Step-by-step process
1. Define exactly who you can help and who already wants the result.
2. Look for people with an active problem and buying energy.
3. Gather their words through surveys, DMs, comments, calls, forms, or applications.
4. Identify what keeps them up at night, what they are afraid of, and what they secretly want.
5. Use those words back in the copy.
6. Position the offer as the answer to that specific person’s prayer, not as a general solution for everyone.
7. Let every headline, story section, bullet, and CTA speak to that one person.

### Example
Weak who-first application:
“I help everyone improve their business.”

Strong who-first application:
“I help coaches, mentors, and experts who already know they can help people but are stuck turning that expertise into a clear offer that consistently gets clients.”

When the “who” sharpens, the copy sharpens automatically. Headlines become easier. Stories become truer. Calls to action become more believable.

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“You have to niche down specifically as to a specific problem that people have. That’s in a specific lane.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“You need to understand them deeply as a person better than they know themselves.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“The best way to do this is by using their own words back to them.”

## How Jeremiah Judges Whether Copy Is Good

Jeremiah does not judge copy primarily by grammar, sophistication, or even originality. He judges it by emotional pull. Does it create movement? Does it sound alive? Does it feel like buried treasure to the right person? Does it make the reader want to keep going?

He uses intuition after structure. First the formula must be present. Then the copy must emotionally hit.

### Name
The Emotional Pull Test

### Step-by-step process
1. Read the copy aloud.
2. Ask whether it sounds like a real invitation or a stiff announcement.
3. Notice whether it creates excitement, relief, urgency, or recognition.
4. Delete any line that feels too hard, too clever, too informational, or too bland.
5. Keep only the lines that tug emotionally.
6. Test whether the copy makes the prospect feel like they found the answer they were hoping for.

### Example
A line like “Join this free workshop to gain clarity” may be technically correct but emotionally weak.

A stronger version is: “If you know you’re meant to help people but you keep getting stuck trying to package it, price it, and explain it—this is where it starts to click.”

The second version creates emotional recognition.

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“I look at sales copy as an art piece that I’m judging based on how it makes me feel more than anything else.”

### Jeremiah’s exact quote
“A well written sales letter feels like somebody found buried treasure inside of their heart.”
