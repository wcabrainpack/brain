## 1. THE IMPERFECT ACTION DOCTRINE

### Core Concept
Jeremiah's entire business philosophy rests on a single non-negotiable behavior: **take action before you feel ready**. The desire to get it perfect, to wait until everything is figured out, to feel comfortable before moving — this is the #1 business killer he sees in coaches every single day.

"Imperfect action" isn't recklessness. It's the recognition that overthinking and over-preparing are forms of fear wearing the costume of wisdom.

### How Jeremiah Teaches It
He distinguishes between two types of people:
- **The Processor:** Someone who thinks through every scenario, weighs every risk, waits for the right moment — and stays stuck.
- **The Actor:** Someone who moves on intuition and instinct, makes quick decisions with incomplete information, and builds momentum through volume of action.

Jeremiah is explicit: in marketing, in sales, in building your business, "reckless action as quickly as you come up with the ideas, not taking a ton of time to process through it is going to make you more successful than you ever imagined."

### The Evidence He Cites
> "I can look back at my life. I can look back at my business. It's those are the times when I've been the most successful financially. I've had the most fulfillment. And the times when I'm processing through and trying to make sure I get it right, those are when I've struggled. Those are when I've had disappointment."

He also notes that having two kids forced this habit on him: "I don't have time to process and think through every little thing or I wouldn't have a life. I wouldn't have a business. I wouldn't have the family that I want. And so I had to create and rebuild myself mentally, emotionally to a place where I'm somebody who takes action quickly."

### How to Apply It
1. When you have an idea — launch it within 48 hours in some form
2. Don't wait to have your full program built before you sell it
3. Put up the offer, the funnel, the lead magnet — imperfect and live beats perfect and in your head
4. Track action volume, not quality of each action
5. Lower the bar for "ready" — if you have something that helps people, it's ready enough

### The Trap It Addresses
The coach who says "I need to build out all my course modules before I take clients." The coach who says "I'll run ads when my landing page is perfect." This is the perfectionism prison — and it masquerades as professionalism.

## 2. THE 6 KEYS TO SELLING MORE PROGRAMS: FROM SELF-ESTEEM TO KILLING PERFECTIONISM

### Core Concept
Jeremiah teaches that the inability to sell is rarely a skills problem — it's a self-esteem and identity problem. The way a coach shows up energetically in a sales conversation tells the prospect everything. Fear of judgment, need for approval, desperation for the sale — these leak out and repel buyers.

He identifies six interconnected keys:

### Key 1: Stop Trying to Impress People
> "The worst thing that you can do when trying to get a sale is to try to impress people. Is to try to get people to like you."

Trying to impress people means you've made the sale about YOUR needs (validation, approval) instead of THEIR needs (transformation). People can feel when they're being pitched at vs. connected with.

### Key 2: Build Trust Before You Pitch
> "You got to build trust. They have to believe in you, your product, your service, your company, and the transformation that you're promising."

Trust is built through consistent content, authentic conversations, and showing up without agenda. Every piece of content Jeremiah creates is designed to connect, not to sell directly.

### Key 3: Let Go of Controlling Their Perception
> "You got to get it out of your head trying to like get people to like you, trying to get them to trust you, trying to get them to believe in you, trying to control their perception of you."

This is subtle. The coach who says "I don't want to seem pushy" is actually prioritizing how they look over whether the prospect gets help. Ironically, letting go of controlling perception makes you MORE trustworthy.

### Key 4: Be Confident in What You Offer
> "I don't give a bleep about what you think about me right now. You can think whatever you want. I'm also confident in what I offer. And I also know that this works."

Confidence isn't arrogance — it's the certainty that your offer genuinely helps people. If you believe that, not selling it is actually doing your prospects a disservice.

### Key 5: Don't Hide the Fact That You Have Something for Sale
> "I don't hide the fact that I have an offer, that I offer something to people that's going to change their life and their business if they jump in. I don't hide that. I don't try to pretend like that doesn't exist. I'm very upfront about it, very open about it. And that's what you want to do as well because that shows honesty, that builds trust right there, right from the get-go."

Being transparent about your offer at every stage — content, conversation, email — is a form of integrity. Hiding it is the actual deception.

### Key 6: Kill Perfectionism in Your Promises
The biggest self-esteem block Jeremiah sees: coaches afraid to promise the BIG result because they might not deliver it for everyone. But this is backwards.

> "You should promise something big and then you should deliver that big thing to people. And listen, not everybody — because you can't guarantee — you can't hold a gun to somebody's head and make sure that they actually do the thing that you say that you're going to do."

In marketing, you promise the outcome the TOP 1% of your clients achieve when they do everything right. Not the average result. The aspirational result.

## 3. DO IT SCARED / DECISION VELOCITY — THE FRAMEWORK

### Core Concept
"Doing it scared" is Jeremiah's phrase for the peak performance skill of taking action BEFORE your nervous system says it's safe to do so. Decision Velocity is the speed at which you move from idea → decision → action.

### The Full Framework

**Step 1: Acknowledge You're Scared**
Don't bypass the emotion. Don't suppress it. Feel it, acknowledge it.
> "Often times, I'm not waiting until I feel comfortable. I will go through a process of calming my nervous system down. Uh, but at the end of the day, sometimes you just have to do it scared. Sometimes you just have to do it uncomfortable."

**Step 2: Compress the Timeline**
Most coaches wait until they have "no other option" before taking uncomfortable action. Jeremiah flips this:
> "What if you just shifted the timeline where instead of waiting until you have no other option, you did that right away."

**Step 3: Build the Behavior into Your Identity**
This isn't about one brave moment. It's about becoming a person who operates this way habitually.
> "You develop that as not just a habit, a behavior, a personality, and a way that you do everything... and you shift into a place of being a person who takes quick action without having it all figured out."

**Step 4: Operate on Instinct Over Analysis**
> "You start moving on intuition and instinct more than, well, I spent all this time processing and logically putting all the pieces together."

### Why Decision Velocity Compounds
Each fast decision you make builds evidence that you CAN act without certainty. Each avoided decision reinforces the belief that you need certainty to act. The fastest path to confidence is making more decisions, not better decisions.

### The Cost of Slow Decisions in Coaching
- Campaigns never launch
- Offers stay in draft
- Discovery calls never get booked
- Content never gets posted
- Clients who needed you never found you

> "You have so much conviction in the very thing that will make you successful to not do it."

## 4. MISTAKE = DATA MENTAL MODEL

### Core Concept
The most damaging thing a coach can do is treat a mistake as evidence of their inadequacy, rather than as information to act on. Jeremiah draws this model directly from his stock trading practice and from *The Disciplined Trader* by Mark Douglas.

### The Mental Model in Detail
**Old mental model:** Mistake → Shame → Avoidance → Stagnation  
**Jeremiah's mental model:** Mistake → Data → Adjustment → Relaunch

> "Mistakes don't actually exist. They are just data to learn from and grow and to move forward."

### The Hot Stove Principle
> "You're going to touch a few hot stoves and you're going to have to literally go back and touch the hot stove again. But realize that you have a glove on and you have the ability to do so with wisdom and knowledge."

The brain is wired to avoid repeating pain — this is evolutionary. But in business, the ability to re-engage with the same thing that hurt you (a campaign, a sales call, a launch) WITH new data is the defining skill of sustainable growth.

### Jeremiah's Personal Example
He lost $12,000 on an options trade in one day. He didn't quit. He applied the "mistakes are data" model, made adjustments, and generated $40,000 in profit the following month. The same principle applied to a $3,000 loss later that week — he was back trading profitably the next day.

> "Every time that you make a mistake is still part of your momentum because you actually did something. Because you actually got into action. You took action. You did something. And that's more than most people can say."

### Shawn White Reference
Jeremiah cites snowboarder Shawn White's philosophy: "I can either win or I can learn from this." White suffered a devastating public injury in the Olympics, got back up, and won a gold medal. He's never used the word "failure" about himself in performance.

### Application for Coaches
- A Facebook ad that flopped → Data. What was the hook? The audience? The offer?
- A launch that didn't convert → Data. What was missing from the sequence?
- A sales call where they said no → Data. Where did the conversation go sideways?
- A client who quit → Data. What was their real objection to taking action?

### The 15-Minute Rule
Jeremiah used to take days to recover from setbacks. Now he gives himself 15 minutes to feel it fully, then snaps back:
> "I've gotten to the point now where it's like, okay, 15 minutes. I can feel it for 15 minutes and I can immediately snap back into where I need to be."

## 5. THE SELF-BELIEF → SALES CONVERSION CONNECTION

### Core Concept
Jeremiah teaches that sales conversion rates are a direct reflection of the coach's internal state. Confident, unattached energy converts. Desperate, needy energy repels. This isn't motivational fluff — it's energetic reality in human interaction.

### Why People Buy
> "The reason why people buy from you is because they trust that they are going to get the result that you're talking about. And it's big enough of a change and a transformation for them. That the pain of leaving behind what they've been experiencing is less than staying in what they're experiencing."

Sales is NOT:
- Convincing
- Persuading
- Impressing
- Closing

Sales IS:
- Imparting emotion and belief
- Building trust
- Mirroring desires back to the prospect
- Helping them see what's possible

### The Authenticity Shortcut
> "If you can literally just be yourself, casual... the version of yourself that shows up on Saturday watching football, drinking a beer if that's what you like to do. That version of you. Seriously, like the version of you that's sitting around at family dinner talking to a long friend. That's the version to show up with."

The "professional" version of most coaches is their LEAST effective selling version. Casual, warm, confident-but-not-pushy converts at dramatically higher rates.

### The Energy Mirror Effect
In a sales conversation, Jeremiah deliberately uses energy mirroring:
1. Match the prospect's energy level at the start
2. Gradually increase your energy and enthusiasm
3. They begin mirroring YOUR energy
4. Their excitement about the transformation builds naturally
5. The sale happens as an energetic conclusion, not a hard push

### "Why Not Me? Why Not Now?"
Jeremiah's internal mantra in every sales interaction:
> "Why not me? And why not now? Oftentimes, we're trying to be way too passive and appease other people when we're selling or in a sales interaction."

This mindset shift — from "I hope they buy" to "why wouldn't they buy, I'm the obvious choice" — transforms close rates.

## 6. THE GROUP COACHING MODEL — STRUCTURE, PRICING, HOW CALLS WORK

### Core Concept
Group coaching is the foundation of Jeremiah's business model. It allows one coach to serve many clients simultaneously, creating both scale and recurring revenue. He built a music coaching business using this model to between $400K–$1M/month.

### Program Structure
A group coaching program typically includes:
- **Weekly coaching calls** — live Zoom sessions where the coach works with the group
- **Course content** — training modules accessible on-demand (doesn't need to be 100% built before launch)
- **Community access** — Facebook group, Discord, or similar for peer support
- **Direct access to the coach** — Q&A, review of work (sales copy, ads, messaging)
- **Resources and templates** — swipe files, scripts, checklists

> "It's access to your training. It's access to coaching with you. It's access to resources that people get. And you don't need to have them all built before you launch something like this."

### The Key Insight: Launch Before It's Ready
This connects directly to the Imperfect Action Doctrine. You sell the program, then build the content based on what clients actually need. This prevents wasted work on content nobody wants and ensures the program is relevant.

### WCA As the Model

## 7. THE PRICING LADDER: FROM $4.95 FRONT-END TO $15K+ CONSULTING

### Core Concept
Jeremiah has a precise, battle-tested pricing architecture built from 22+ years of coaching business experience, including scaling to $1M/month. The key insight: pricing is not just about revenue — it's a qualification system.

> "The more you charge, the less people are going to come in, but also they're going to be more qualified and less headaches."

### The Full Pricing Ladder

**Tier 1: Lead-In Offer — $4.95**
- Not free. The small price tag is deliberate.
- Filters out unserious people who waste your time
- Qualifies buyers for higher-tier programs
- The algorithm rewards this price point in paid advertising
- At $4.95, your ad spend might be $10 per registration (vs. $200 for a $100 offer)
- Does NOT make you money — it QUALIFIES your buyers

> "I'm actually leaning towards doing that more than you doing a free lead magnet anymore. Free lead magnets, especially if you're running advertising, you know, you're losing money each lead that you get, whereas you can make back a little bit of that, plus you're qualifying people better."

**Tier 2: Entry Group Program — $47–$97/month**
- For coaches just starting out
- $47/month = ~$570/year per member (not much, but sustainable)
- $97/month = ~$1,164/year per member
- Provides recurring revenue and relationship-building
- This is where you prove your model

**Tier 3: Mid-Tier Group Program — $150–$197/month**
- For coaches with experience, testimonials, and momentum
- $197/month = ~$2,364/year per member
- Target once you have a working program and results to show
- Annual equivalent: ~$2,000/year

**Tier 4: High-Ticket Elite Program — $800–$900/month**
- ~$9,600/year per member at $800/month
- "An elite program that people can upgrade from this level into this program to get more access with you, more commitment"
- This is where your profit margins dramatically expand
- Serves clients who want transformation, not just information

**Tier 5: Consulting/VIP — $10,000–$15,000+ (implied)**
- Jeremiah references $15,000 consulting work
- One-on-one access, custom strategy, deepest level of support
- Only for highly qualified, serious clients
- Not necessary early on — earn your way up to this

### The Revenue Math That Proves It
> "We actually use this exact model to build a music coaching business to over a million dollars per month... just from the coaching business alone, it did right around $400,000 a month, about between $400 and $600,000 a month."

Mechanics: 100+ people at the $1,000/month tier PLUS multiple lower tiers stacked = compounding recurring revenue.

### Critical Principle: Add High Ticket to Expand Margins
> "It's hard to be able to create the kind of sustainable profit that you want on just a $47 program on just even a $197 program. So, adding high ticket offers in there helps you expand your profit margins into that."

### Don't Be the Cheapest Option
Never race to the bottom on pricing. Underpricing signals low value and attracts low-commitment clients.
> "If you're underpricing, people aren't going to take you seriously."

## 8. THE NAVIGATOR / TOUR GUIDE POSITIONING

### Core Concept
One of the biggest mental blocks new coaches have is: "I'm not the world's greatest expert on this topic. Who am I to charge money?"

Jeremiah dismantles this with the Tour Guide positioning: **you don't have to be the best in the world at something to guide others through it. You just have to be further down the road.**

### The Core Teaching
> "What's your lane. What's that area that people want to come to you for? They want to work with you for. They want to learn from you for and discover what's possible when you do that."

A coach is not a professor. A coach is a navigator — someone who has been where the client is trying to go, knows the route, and can guide them through it step by step.

### The Integrity Trap
Many coaches tie their coaching authority to whether they're "the best" at their topic. This is backwards. Your clients aren't paying for world-class expertise — they're paying for:
1. Accountability
2. A clear path forward
3. Your specific experience and proven system
4. Your presence and belief in them

### Why This Matters for Bold Marketing
Jeremiah connects this to the caution problem:
> "I find that people with a lot of skill, a lot of talent, a lot of ability. They want to do things right and they want to do things ethical and they want to do things with integrity. And by the way, none of that's bad or wrong. Here's the problem. Because of their need and desire for integrity, they also put an inordinate fear on people judging them."

The result: coaches undersell, underpromise, and underperform — not because they're bad coaches, but because they're afraid to claim authority they've earned.

### The "Fraud Police" Fear
> "Inside of them, they have something where they're afraid of the fraud police scrutinizing them saying, 'Well, you said I was going to be able to reach a six-figure goal and you only taught me the first step of how to get started, so you lied to me when you created an offer that promised that.' No, that's completely ridiculous."

Jeremiah has created hundreds of offers. Nobody has ever accused him of fraud for promising a big outcome while delivering the first steps.

## 9. THE GRADUATION LADDER: COACHING → COURSES → CONSULTING

### Core Concept
Jeremiah's business architecture creates a natural ascension path for clients AND a natural diversification path for the coach's income. Nobody starts at the top — you climb the ladder together.

### The Three Rungs

**Rung 1: Group Coaching**
- Starting point for most coaches
- Weekly calls, group community, basic content library
- Lower price point, higher volume
- Proves your system works
- Builds your testimonials and case studies

**Rung 2: Courses**
- Packaged version of your coaching content
- Scalable: sell to 1 or 10,000 with the same effort
- Passive income component
- Deepens the relationship with people who've already bought from you
- "Streamline creating the content for your program so that you're not taking all this time"

**Rung 3: Consulting**
- Premium, personalized, high-touch
- $10K–$15K+ engagements
- Requires established authority and proven results
- For the most serious clients with the most resources
- Highest income per hour spent

### The Key Insight: Build In Order
Don't try to launch a $15K consulting offer before you have group coaching clients. The coaching experience:
- Teaches you what clients actually need
- Generates testimonials
- Builds your reputation
- Creates the case studies that justify consulting rates

### Jeremiah's Own Journey
He built WCA (group coaching) first, which generates recurring revenue and regular client interaction, then layered in higher-ticket consulting based on what he was seeing work inside the group.

## 10. DISCOVERY CALL PROTOCOL — QUESTIONS, RED FLAGS, HOW TO DISQUALIFY

### Core Concept
A discovery call (or sales call) is not a pitch — it's a qualification conversation. Jeremiah's approach inverts the typical sales dynamic: instead of trying to convince people to buy, you listen deeply, reflect back, and let the prospect sell themselves.

### The One-on-One Sales Sequence

**Phase 1: Casual Connection**
Start with questions about their life, not their business problem.
> "Ask the questions about their family, what they like to do for fun, what they do for work. And usually if you just start a conversation casually, they're going to give you one, two, or three of those things, and they're going to start to talk about them themselves."

Don't jump straight to pain points. Build rapport first. The "football Saturday" version of you, not the "professional presenter" version.

**Phase 2: Unpack Their Desires**
Guide the conversation toward what they WANT, not just what they're struggling with.
> "You want to guide it to what your message is and you want to guide it towards listening to what their desires are. You want them to tell you what they desire. What do they want more than anything else?"

**Phase 3: Mirror Their Energy**
Match their emotional intensity, then gradually increase yours.
> "I will mirror the person's energy when I start and then I will increase my energy so that they start mirroring my energy and excitement. And then that imparts to them what I'm feeling and my belief that I have and what I offer."

**Phase 4: Reflect Back Their Desires**
Literally repeat back to them, in their words, what they told you they want.
> "I know that you said that you want to go on a vacation with your family and that you're struggling by having to work so many hours... What if you could make a lasting change in your life, your family's life, and your future?"

**Phase 5: Present the Offer as the Bridge**
The offer is not a product — it's the path from their current pain to their stated desire. Frame it that way.

### Red Flags — When to Disqualify
Jeremiah implicitly identifies these through his pricing and qualification architecture:
- Unwilling to pay even $4.95 for a lead-in (filters out freeloaders at the top of funnel)
- Complaining about price before hearing the value
- Looking for a magic fix without personal accountability
- Clients who "literally will not do what I teach" — Jeremiah references these explicitly

> "Not every client that I have doesn't even make six figures because some of them literally will not do what I teach."

This is NOT a coaching failure — it's a qualification failure. Better discovery calls catch this earlier.

### Using AI to Prepare and Follow Up
Jeremiah's $20K deal closed through AI-assisted follow-up:
> "I had a client where I did a soft sales pitch without a price on a conversation. I pulled the transcript in because I recorded it and I actually pulled the transcript right into chat GPT and I told it, okay, take this transcript and turn it into a proposal email and write me some bullet points for a proposal video to send to the person. And it literally closed the sale, made $20,000 right off of that."

Use AI to:
- Draft the post-call follow-up email from call notes
- Prepare opening questions before the call
- Create a custom proposal script from the conversation
- Write a re-engagement text if they go quiet after expressing interest

## 11. THE ACCOUNTABILITY ARCHITECTURE — COACHING CLIENTS WHO WON'T TAKE ACTION

### Core Concept
Every coach eventually faces this: a client who has everything they need to succeed, but won't take action. Jeremiah addresses this head-on, both as a business reality and a mindset challenge.

### The Emotional Resilience Foundation
Before you can hold clients accountable, you need to be able to handle the emotional weight of watching someone stay stuck. If their inaction devastates you, you'll enable them.

> "If you can learn to be stronger in the midst of those situations, you can accomplish anything you imagined as a business owner, as a mentor, as a service provider."

Jeremiah's model: feel the frustration for 15 minutes, then approach the situation with curiosity instead of judgment.

### What Non-Action Is Really About
People who "won't take action" are usually:
1. Tethered to a past failure they haven't resolved
2. Afraid of a specific outcome (success OR failure)
3. Trying to be too perfect before moving
4. Lacking the decision velocity framework

> "Imagine that you have a chain that's tethering you to a previous experience or a memory or a moment in your past that's replaying itself. And so you're tethered so much to that memory in your past that will not let you keep going because you have not resolved that."

### How to Coach Through It
1. **Name the pattern without shame.** "I'm noticing you've been stuck at this step for three weeks. Let's talk about what's actually in the way."
2. **Ask about past failures.** What happened the last time they tried something like this?
3. **Reduce the action.** If they won't take the big step, what's the smallest possible next move?
4. **Apply the Mistake = Data reframe.** Help them see the attempted action — whatever the result — as valuable data, not evidence of failure.
5. **Don't rescue them.** Your job is to hold the container, not carry them through it.
6. **If nothing works, refer out.** Sometimes a therapist needs to untangle the underlying belief before the coaching can work.

> "Sometimes we need to work with a therapist through what is the underlying issue beneath the surface that's causing this to keep coming up."

### The Coach's Own Accountability
Jeremiah is clear: you can't coach emotional resilience and imperfect action if you don't embody them yourself. Clients feel your energy. If you're afraid of their failure, they'll feel that too.

## 12. THE NICHE-DOWN = SPECIALIZATION WEALTH PRINCIPLE

### Core Concept
Jeremiah's consistent thread across all his content: the coach who serves everyone serves no one. Niching down is not a limitation — it's the mechanism through which expertise, authority, and premium pricing become possible.

### Why Generalists Stay Poor
A general "life coach" or "business coach" competes with 100,000 others. They're a commodity. Pricing is a race to the bottom. Marketing messages are vague. Leads are unqualified. Conversions are low.

A coach who works specifically with "recently divorced women over 45 who want to start an online coaching business" has:
- A clear ideal client avatar
- Messaging that resonates deeply with a specific pain
- Authority in a specific niche
- Higher conversion rates because every word is targeted
- Premium pricing justified by specialization

### The "What's Your Lane" Test
> "Figure out what's your lane. What's that area that people want to come to you for? They want to work with you for. They want to learn from you for and discover what's possible when you do that."

Your lane is:
1. Something you have genuine results in
2. Something you can credibly teach
3. Something a specific, definable audience needs
4. Something you can talk about for years without burning out

### Specialization Creates the Pricing Ladder
Once you're known as THE person for a specific problem, you can charge:
- $4.95 for the entry point to your world
- $97/month for ongoing access
- $900/month for your elite program
- $15K+ for direct consulting

Generalists can't charge these rates. Specialists can.

### The Bold Promise Requires a Clear Niche
Jeremiah's marketing principle — "promise the outcome the top 1% of your clients get" — only works if you can clearly identify WHO your clients are and WHAT outcome they're seeking.

> "You want to promise the ultimate outcome. Even if somebody invested months and months or even years with you, you want to promise that outcome that only the top elite 1% of your clients get."

A vague audience means a vague promise means a weak offer means poor conversions. Niche → Specific Promise → Strong Offer → High Conversions.

### The Stoic Business Mindset: Adversity as Advantage
Jeremiah grounds his entire philosophy in stoic resilience, referencing *The Obstacle Is the Way* (Ryan Holiday) and Marcus Aurelius's *Meditations*. The principle: what blocks you IS the path. The failed campaign is training. The client who quit is information. The technology problem is a skill-building opportunity.

> "Learning to grow and strengthen myself in my business, my ability to think, my ability to operate and find the good in every situation is powerful."

This isn't toxic positivity. It's the business owner's operating system: everything that happens, happens FOR your growth, not TO your detriment.
