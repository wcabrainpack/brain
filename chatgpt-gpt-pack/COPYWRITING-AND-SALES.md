## The Hierarchy of Copy Work

1. **WHO first** — Define the specific person before writing a single word
2. **Their words, not yours** — Survey, comments, DMs → mirror their exact language back
3. **Outcome headline** — Lead with the promised result, not the process
4. **Story section** — 2-3 paragraphs of emotional narrative BEFORE the offer
5. **Offer stack** — What's included (outcomes, not features)
6. **Social proof** — Specific numbers only (not "many people have transformed")
7. **CTA** — Direct, confident, no apology

## The Rewrite Method (Jeremiah's #1 Copy Weapon)

**The exact ChatGPT/Claude prompt Jeremiah uses:**
> "Rewrite this sales copy with the ideas of Dan Kennedy and Gary Halbert and Sandy Krakowski in mind. Keep the same context, don't remove any copy, and write a final sales letter."

**What each voice brings:**
- **Gary Halbert:** Conversational, news-focused hook ("Fellow American...")
- **Dan Kennedy:** Direct response bullets, fascination copy, no wasted words, authority positioning
- **Sandy Krakowski:** Connection, empathy, warmth, faith-forward energy

**When to use:** Any time existing copy feels flat, generic, or underperforming.

**Key rules:**
- Minimum price point: $500–$1,000 for paid ads to be profitable (don't write copy for $100 offers)
- Social proof must have SPECIFIC numbers: "$40K in 30 days" not "amazing results"
- Urgency must be REAL — attached to an actual deadline, not fake countdown timers

## Landing Page Anatomy (5-Section Formula)

**In exact order:**

### Section 1: Headline
- Short. Bold. Large. One promised outcome.
- Formula: **[Result they want] even if [their main objection]**
- Example: *"You will be able to earn six figures even if you've never sold anything before."*

### Section 2: Story Section (THE MOST SKIPPED, MOST IMPORTANT)
- 2–3 paragraphs. Emotional and relatable BEFORE the offer.
- Include a real photo of you.
- Formula: Agitate pain → Empathize → Tease the solution
- Fifth-grade reading level. Short paragraph breaks. Skimmable.
- Jeremiah: *"99% of people miss the story section. The story section alone decides whether people keep reading."*

### Section 3: What's Included
- Bullet points of OUTCOMES, not features.
- Wrong: "8 module course"
- Right: "You'll know exactly what to say to get a new client on your next sales call"
- Optional soft CTA midway through.

### Section 4: Testimonials
- Specific numbers only. Name + outcome.
- For high-ticket, place BEFORE "What's Included"
- Your own story substitutes if you have no testimonials yet.

### Section 5: Call to Action
- Final purchase/register link.
- Optional brief bio below it.
- White background, black text. Simple. Fast.

**Jeremiah's Quote:**
> "There's no single greatest aspect to marketing your business than the landing page copy... The number one lever you can pull that makes the biggest impact."

## The Story Hook Formula

```
"Are you tired of [PAIN/PROBLEM]?
I know what it's like. I've been there.
[2-3 sentences of struggle narrative].
And then I found [SOLUTION]."
```

**Key rules:**
- Agitate BEFORE the solution — never skip straight to fix
- Show them in the struggle first ("you want them to see themselves")
- Fifth-grade reading level
- Short paragraphs that scan in 3 seconds
- Never mention price in the story section

## Sales Copy Principles (From Live Rewrite Sessions)

### The One Problem / One Solution Rule
Your marketing should point at **one specific problem** and **one key solution**. Fire-hosing people with everything is the #1 trust-killer.

**Jeremiah:** *"You could have the best program, the best information, but if you're trying to check all the boxes and give people all the right information without specifically pointing out one problem and one key solution — you're going to miss it. This is the secret sauce."*

### Sell What They Want, Deliver What They Need
- **Marketing:** Lead with what they WANT (the vacation, the transformation, the outcome)
- **Delivery:** Give them what they NEED (mindset, process, hard work)
- This is NOT bait-and-switch — the destination is real; the road is harder than they expected

### Specificity Sells
- "Gut health for women 40+" vs. "health coaching" → 10x the conversion
- "Make $10K/mo in 90 days" vs. "grow your business" → specific beats vague every time

### The Swipe File + AI Remix Method
1. Maintain a dedicated email account for competitor/marketer emails (15 years of swipe = Jeremiah's advantage)
2. Sign up for competitors' email lists by clicking their ads
3. When writing copy, pull 2-3 emails that match the tone/offer type
4. Feed to Claude: *"Rewrite the following email to promote [YOUR OFFER], using the above as the framework for tone, style, and cadence"*
5. Edit the output in your own voice
6. Launch → feedback → iterate

## Sales Calls (One-on-One)

### The Core Principle
> "Sales is not convincing someone to buy. It's imparting emotion, belief, and logic in that order."

### The Sales Conversation Sequence
1. **Mirror their energy at the start** — match their pace, then gradually raise yours
2. **Ask and listen** — family, goals, what they want, what's blocking them
3. **Mirror their desires back** — use their exact words: "You said you want X. What if you could have that?"
4. **Be confident, not pushy** — "Desperation is the perfume of the broke. Get it out of your energy."
5. **Don't hide the offer** — Be upfront about what you have. Hiding it kills trust.
6. **The close** — "I know we can help you. Here's what the next step looks like."

### Disqualify First
Before you try to sell, look for reasons this person is NOT a fit. This:
- Preserves your energy for right-fit clients
- Eliminates high-pressure tactics (you genuinely want to find the right person)
- Paradoxically INCREASES conversions because you come off confident, not desperate

**Red flags to disqualify immediately:**
- "Every coach I've had has screwed me over"
- Asks for money-back guarantee before you even pitch
- Evasive in discovery call
- Not willing to invest anything in their own growth

### Sales Is Service
> "Sales is service. You don't need to pressure somebody into signing up... As long as you can find people already saying yes to your offer, you don't need high-pressure tactics."

When you genuinely believe your program will change someone's life, selling feels like doing them a favor.

## Pricing Copy Rules

### Price Anchoring
Always present a higher price first, then the lower real price.
> "Normally this program is $997. Today, because you're on this webinar, the investment is just $197/mo."

### The Pricing Ladder Copy
Frame each tier as a qualifier, not just a price:
- $5–$10: "This small investment shows you're serious"
- $197/mo: "This is your commitment to getting results"
- $900/mo: "This is reserved for people who want 1:1 strategic focus"
- $3.5k+: "This is for people who want done-for-you results"

### The ROI Frame
Sell with ROI context whenever possible:
- "One client from this training pays for a full year of membership"
- "At $197/mo, you need one new client to make this free"
- "Most members make this back in 30-60 days"

## The Facebook Ad Copy Formula

### Hook Formula (0-3 seconds):
Pattern interrupt → Relevant question or bold claim → No preamble

Examples:
- "Are you a coach or mentor who's tired of chasing clients?"
- "This is what $40K in 30 days looks like when you specialize..."
- "Most coaches make ONE mistake that kills their sales. Here's what it is."

### Body:
- Agitate the pain (2-3 sentences)
- Introduce the solution (1-2 sentences)  
- Social proof (one specific number)
- CTA to register/learn more

### Rules:
- No "link in bio" — direct link in copy for cold traffic
- Ask a question in the hook to qualify the audience
- Polarizing hooks outperform safe hooks — repel non-buyers, attract buyers
- Video > image for cold traffic. But test both.

## Hot Seat / Live Copy Review Notes (From WCA Rewrite Days)

Recurring patterns from 4 years of live copy critiques:

1. **Most headlines are too vague** — "Transform Your Life" = zero conversion. Specific outcome = buyers.
2. **Story sections are missing** — Most coaches jump straight to "Here's what you get." Fatal.
3. **Copy is about the coach, not the client** — "I've been doing this 20 years" ≠ "Here's exactly what you'll be able to do."
4. **Testimonials have no numbers** — "She was amazing!" ≠ "I made $14K in 30 days using her system."
5. **CTA has no urgency** — "Click here if you're interested" → "Registration closes Friday at midnight — 7 spots left."
6. **Sentences are too long** — Fifth-grade reading level. If you can't scan it in 3 seconds, cut it.

**Jeremiah on feedback:**
> "When I review your copy, I ask: Would I buy this? Does this make me feel something? Does the story feel real? If the answer is no — we rewrite the headline first."
