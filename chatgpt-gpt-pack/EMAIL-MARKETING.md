## The 3 Types of Emails You Need

### 1. Broadcast Emails (Daily)
One-time sends to your full list. Use for:
- Daily value/content (story → insight → CTA)
- Promoting YouTube/podcast content
- Announcing events, offers, deadlines

### 2. Autoresponder Series (Evergreen)
Pre-written emails triggered when someone joins your list.
Template sequence:
- **Day 1:** Welcome + give them what they signed up for + soft intro to your coaching
- **Day 2:** Your story (your mess → your message)
- **Day 3:** Teaching email (one specific insight from your framework)
- **Day 4:** Social proof (one specific client result with numbers)
- **Day 5:** Offer pitch (your group coaching program) + urgency
- **Days 6–7:** 2-3 follow-up pitches with different angles + hard deadline

### 3. Event Series Emails
Before and after every live event:
- **5 days before:** "Here's what you'll learn"
- **2 days before:** "Your spot is reserved — are you ready?"
- **Day of — morning:** "Today is the day"
- **Day of — 1 hour before:** "Starting in 1 hour"
- **Day of — 15 min before:** "We go live in 15 min"
- **Day of — at start:** "We're live"
- **Day after:** Recording + offer with deadline
- **Day 2 after:** Last chance to watch + join
- **Final 3 hours:** 3 separate emails on the deadline

## How to Write Emails (Jeremiah's Method)

### The Structure: Story → Bridge → CTA

Every email has this arc:
1. **Story/hook** (1-3 sentences that pull them in — a relatable struggle, an interesting question, a bold claim)
2. **Bridge** (connect the story to your insight or offer)
3. **CTA** (one clear action, always)

### The AI Method:
1. Open your swipe file email account (dedicated Gmail with 15 years of competitor/marketer emails)
2. Find an email with the right cadence/tone for what you're writing
3. Copy it into Claude: *"Rewrite the following email to promote [YOUR OFFER]. Make it more persuasive around [PAIN POINT] and promise [ONE OUTCOME]. Keep the same tone and cadence."*
4. Edit the output in your own voice
5. Send

### Writing Style:
- Conversational — write like you talk to a friend, not a client
- Short sentences. Short paragraphs. White space.
- Fifth-grade reading level
- One call to action per email (not 3 links)
- Subject line is the ONLY thing that gets them to open — treat it like the headline

## Subject Line Formulas

**Formula: Curiosity Gap + Benefit + Specificity**

Best performers from Jeremiah's swipe file:

| Type | Example |
|------|---------|
| Question | "Are you making this one email mistake?" |
| Curiosity gap | "The weird subject line that got a 42% open rate" |
| Specificity | "How Sarah made $14K in 30 days (step by step)" |
| Contrast | "Why emailing once a week is killing your list" |
| Personal | "I almost quit in 2023. Here's what stopped me." |
| Challenge | "If you can't answer this question, you won't get clients" |
| Bold claim | "This one email type generates 80% of my coaching signups" |

**Rules:**
- No deceptive clickbait — it kills trust and spikes unsubscribes
- Numbers work ("3 emails that get more clients than webinars")
- Keep it under 50 characters when possible
- The open is won or lost in 2 seconds

## Email Sequences: Templates

### Welcome Sequence (5 Days)

**Day 1 (immediate):**
Subject: "You're in — here's your [THING]"
Body: Short thank you. Deliver the lead magnet or content. Brief intro to who you are. One soft mention of your program.

**Day 2:**
Subject: "My story (the short version)"
Body: Your personal transformation story. Where you were → the turning point → where you are now. End: "I built [PROGRAM NAME] because I know this path works."

**Day 3:**
Subject: "The [NUMBER] mistake that was costing me [PAIN]"
Body: Teaching email. One specific insight from your framework. "Here's what I learned that changed everything." Short. Actionable. Ends with CTA to learn more or book a call.

**Day 4:**
Subject: "[CLIENT NAME] made [SPECIFIC RESULT] — here's how"
Body: One specific client story with numbers. "She came to me with [PROBLEM]. 30 days later, [RESULT]. Here's what we did differently." CTA: "Want the same? Here's how."

**Day 5:**
Subject: "This is your invitation"
Body: Direct pitch for your program. Problem → your solution → what's included → price → CTA. Limited-time offer (72-hour window).

**Days 6–7:**
2-3 follow-up pitches with different angles:
- Objection-handling ("You might be thinking...")
- Urgency ("Closing in 3 hours")
- Social proof ("Here are more results")

### Event Promo Sequence

**5 days before:**
"Here's what we're covering on [DATE]..."
Agenda + who it's for + link to register

**2 days before:**
"[FIRST NAME], we have [X] seats left for [EVENT]"
Social proof from past events + urgency

**Day of — morning:**
"Today is the day — [EVENT NAME] starts at [TIME]"
What to bring / what to prepare + link

**Day of — 1 hour before:**
"Starting in 1 hour — are you ready?"
Quick note. Link. That's it.

**Day of — 15 min:**
"15 minutes — see you there"
One line. Link.

**Post-event — Day 1:**
"Here's the recording [+ special offer]"
Value recap + offer pitch + 72-hour deadline

**Post-event — Day 2:**
"Last chance to watch + join"
Deadline reminder + one strong testimonial

**Last 3 hours — 3 emails:**
- "3 hours left"
- "1 hour left"
- "Closes at midnight — final chance"

## Email Content Ideas (Rotating Topics)

Cycle through these for daily broadcast content:

1. **Client story** — [Client] came to me with [problem]. Here's what we did.
2. **Myth-bust** — "The biggest lie about [topic] in the coaching industry"
3. **Quick tip** — One tactical insight. One action item. One CTA.
4. **Behind the scenes** — What I'm working on, testing, learning
5. **Q&A** — "I got this question yesterday from a member..."
6. **Direct offer** — Today I'm offering [thing]. Here's why. Here's the link.
7. **Story + lesson** — Personal experience → business principle → apply it
8. **Resource recommendation** — Book, tool, or framework I use
9. **Objection handling** — "You might be thinking [objection]. Here's the truth."
10. **Upcoming event reminder** — "Join me [DATE] for [EVENT]"

## What NOT to Do (From Jeremiah's Direct Teaching)

- **Don't email once a week.** By the time Friday rolls around, they forgot they subscribed.
- **Don't write 1,000-word essays.** Get to the point. Busy people scan, not read.
- **Don't use passive CTAs.** "If you're interested, maybe check this out" → no. "Click here to join today" → yes.
- **Don't send from no-reply@.** Use a real name: `jeremiah@jeremiahkrakowski.com`. Personalization matters.
- **Don't send 0 emails during launches.** This is when you should send MORE, not less. People miss emails.
- **Don't forget segmentation.** Sending a "join my program" email to existing members damages trust.
