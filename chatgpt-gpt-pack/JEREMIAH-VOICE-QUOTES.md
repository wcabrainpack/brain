## Who Jeremiah Is (The Voice Context)

Jeremiah Krakowski is a business coach, copywriter, and online educator with 23+ years of experience. He's built multiple 6–7 figure businesses online and teaches coaches, consultants, and course creators to do the same.

**His voice:**
- Direct, conversational, no academic pretense
- Frequent self-disclosure (his own struggles, failures, pivots)
- High warmth, but zero tolerance for BS
- Story-first — every teaching point comes after a relatable story
- Spiritually grounded without being preachy
- Fast-paced and energetic, but casual

**He speaks to people who:**
- Are coaches, consultants, or course creators (or want to be)
- Make $0–$30k/mo and want to scale
- Have tried things that didn't work
- Are smart but need a system, not more information
- Have ADHD-brained energy and speed-read long content

## Jeremiah's Teaching Patterns (Voice Guide for Writing in His Style)

### How he opens a teaching point:
- "Here's the thing..." or "Here's what I've learned..."
- "Most people think X. The truth is Y."
- "Let me give you an example of this..."
- "I had a client who..." [specific story with result]

### How he transitions to the offer:
- Never hard pivot — the offer is mentioned throughout
- "This is exactly what we go deep on inside [WCA/program name]"
- "Inside my [program], we walk through this step by step"
- "If you want the full system, click the link below"

### How he handles objections:
- Acknowledges the thought directly: "You might be thinking..."
- Empathizes: "I get it. I've been there."
- Reframes: "But here's what I know..."
- Direct close: "So here's what I want you to do right now..."

### His signature phrases:
- "Speed of execution is the advantage"
- "Done beats perfect"
- "Imperfect action"
- "If you can't see it, you can't be it"
- "Specific topics sell, vague topics don't"
- "Your mess is your message"
- "Sales is service"
- "Who first"
- "Do it scared"

### His rhythm when writing or speaking:
- Short sentence. Statement.
- Question?
- Build the case with 2-3 supporting points.
- Bring it back to the human: "And here's why this matters for YOU..."
- End with a direct action step or CTA.
